Dodge Charger Build Guide

The Charger can combine four-door practicality with street presence, V8 performance, straight-line speed or a serious audio system. Start with the exact engine, drivetrain, brake package and intended use so every upgrade supports the same goal.

Fitment matters: Charger engines, RWD/AWD drivetrains, trims, brake packages and widebody configurations vary. Verify exact manufacturer fitment, calibration needs and installation requirements before purchasing parts.

Choose your Charger build direction

Street / Daily

Start with dependable tires, brakes and suspension. Wheels, exhaust, lighting and exterior changes can sharpen the car without giving up the comfort that makes a Charger useful every day.

HEMI Performance

Match intake, exhaust, tuning and supporting modifications to the exact engine. Naturally aspirated V8 and factory-supercharged combinations require different planning.

Drag / Straight-Line

Traction, tires, drivetrain condition, cooling and repeatability matter alongside horsepower. Build supporting systems around the intended power level and track use.

AWD / All-Season Daily

For AWD-equipped cars, wheel and tire sizing requires extra care. Maintain appropriate tire compatibility and verify drivetrain-specific fitment before changing the setup.

Blacked-Out / Aggressive

Coordinate wheel width, offset, tire dimensions, ride height and body clearance. Brake packages and standard- versus widebody configurations can change available wheel fitment.

Big Audio

Plan enclosure space, amplifier demand, wiring, grounding and charging capacity as one system while preserving the passenger and cargo space you actually use.

A smarter upgrade order

  1. Baseline condition: address maintenance, tires, brakes, leaks and worn steering or suspension components.
  2. Define the build: street, performance, drag, appearance, audio or a balanced combination.
  3. Tires, wheels and suspension: establish traction, stance and clearance together.
  4. Brakes and supporting systems: prepare for the intended driving and power level.
  5. Powertrain: use engine- and drivetrain-specific modifications and appropriate tuning where required.
  6. Exterior and audio: finish the visual and cabin direction after the mechanical plan is established.

Before buying Charger parts

Confirm year, engine, RWD or AWD drivetrain, trim, brake package and standard- versus widebody configuration where relevant. Wheel research should include bolt pattern, center bore, dimensions, offset, tire sizing, brake clearance and suspension/body clearance. Performance parts must match the exact powertrain and calibration requirements. Marketplace listing titles are research leads—not proof of exact fitment.
